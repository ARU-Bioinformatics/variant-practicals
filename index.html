Genomic variant analysis

Two practicals and a coursework workbench that take you from raw sequencing reads to the meaning of a genetic variant. Everything runs in your web browser – a real command line, a Galaxy-style workflow engine, the IGV genome browser, variant annotation with Ensembl VEP and a 3D protein viewer – with nothing to install.

Before you start

  • Use Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari on a laptop or desktop computer; a wide screen helps.
  • Do the practicals in order: the second starts from the variants you make in the first.
  • Your progress and answers are saved in this browser on this computer. Use My answers on each page to download them.
  • The pages need an internet connection for the data, for Ensembl VEP and for the database links.

What is real, what is simulated?

  • Real: samtools, bcftools, bgzip and tabix (compiled to WebAssembly and run in your browser); the IGV genome browser; Ensembl VEP; the protein structures; all the data.
  • Replayed: FastQC, BWA and FreeBayes are too large for a browser, so the terminal replays their real output on exactly these reads.
  • The workflow engine imitates Galaxy and runs the same programs as the terminal.

Data: 1000 Genomes Project (NA12878, SRR098401); UCSC hg19/GRCh37; NCBI RefSeq; Ensembl; gnomAD; RCSB PDB. Software: samtools/bcftools/htslib via biowasm and Aioli, igv.js, NGL Viewer (MIT licences).
